How is the Lab Score actually built?+
Hitter lane

Talent and power scores come from barrel rate, hard-hit rate, whiff rate and HR Index where the current snapshot has them. Discipline blends walk and chase-adjacent metrics. This lane asks: is this hitter built to do damage on contact?

Pitcher lane

The opposing starter's live strikeout-market line becomes a contact-opportunity proxy — a lower K line implies more balls in play, which raises the floor for any hitter in the lineup, power profile aside.

Market lane

Six-book agreement, price spread and the Short Open probability edge versus consensus round out the picture. Tight agreement means the market has largely converged; wide spread means there is real room to shop the number.

Evidence model: hitter inputs come from the current Short Open HR Index dataset (including barrel rate, hard-hit rate, whiff rate and HR Index where available); opponent contact opportunity uses the starter’s current K-line from the Strikeout Market snapshot; market probability and agreement come from the six-book Home Run Props board. Baseball Savant’s public Statcast framework is the underlying reference for the meaning of barrel, hard-hit, xSLG/quality-of-contact and plate-discipline concepts. When an exact hitter field is missing from the current embedded HR Index snapshot, the Lab fills the display with a clearly marked Short Open proxy (~) so the research surface stays complete; proxies are not represented as official Statcast measurements. Direct matchup history is used only when a tracked event/sample was verified, and is deliberately given a modest weight. The Lab score is a Short Open synthesis — not a claimed Statcast or sportsbook metric.
