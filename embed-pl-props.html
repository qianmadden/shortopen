A call, not a coin flip. Every posted price is stripped of its margin and measured against what the rest of the market says about the same player, with each side's expected goals read straight off the result market. Where they disagree, there is an edge. Where they don't, we say PASS.

How The Call Is MadeEvery number on a card is a price somebody posted. The question each card answers is narrow: is the longest price on the board longer than the rest of the market says it should be?
LevelThree-way result price

Chelsea 1.91 and Tottenham 1.03 expected goals, solved from the de-vigged 1X2 alone (Chelsea -150, draw +310, Spurs +350): three fair probabilities pin both Poisson means exactly. It sizes clean sheets and frames every card; it does not price a player.

ModelThe rest of the market

A book is graded against what the other books post for the same player, de-vigged - never against an average that includes its own price. Each book is also scaled to how it usually prices against the others, because one column here runs a median 17% longer on everybody; that is its habit, not twenty-five separate mistakes.

Fair priceThe best book, de-vigged

What the longest price says once its margin is taken off, using the site's one-sided rule (the home-run board's 4.5% hold). Edge vs fair is the model minus that number: how much more the rest of the market rates the player than the book paying the most.

GradingDisagreement moved against it

The books stray from their own habit by a measured 18.5% on a typical row. A call has to survive half of that moved against it: STRONG at +2% EV or better on three books, LEAN at +0.5% on two. A row more than twice that far from the rest is not graded - without book names it cannot be told apart from team news.

Where the prices come fromHonest accounting

OddsIndex aggregates the major US books, DraftKings and FanDuel among them; each row carries the one to three prices it lists, and the card shows the best of them. The one-sided de-vig is light - true margins are wider - so read EV as a ceiling. Clean sheet has no posted price and is ShortOpen’s own number.
