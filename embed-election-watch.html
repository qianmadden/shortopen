The ShortOpen Election Model

Two numbers, on purpose. The Consensus Read translates where independent race-rating outlets (Cook Political Report, Ballotpedia, Sabato's Crystal Ball) currently have each race into a single number — a citation, not a ShortOpen opinion. Ratings outlets describe races in labels — Safe, Likely, Lean, Tilt, Toss-up — and ShortOpen converts those labels onto one consistent numeric scale so every race can be compared side by side.

The ShortOpen Read is a separate model, built independently from those labels. It weighs a district's full multi-cycle voting history rather than just its most recent result, applies a re-election base rate adjusted for how close the incumbent's last win actually was, and factors in the fundraising gap between campaigns. It also runs on a short, deliberate delay: a rating shift made in the last 60 days is held at half-weight until a second independent signal — a new poll, a fundraising filing, a subsequent ratings change — confirms it. Momentum gets time to prove itself before the model moves with it.

That's “know the open, short the consensus” applied to politics. On most races the two numbers land close together, which is a normal, healthy outcome, not a failure of either method. When they diverge by a meaningful margin, that gap is flagged directly on the card — and it's worth reading the reasoning behind it before deciding which number to trust.
